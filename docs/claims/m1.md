**Sensitivity grid summary**: Under our documented assumptions, the reject-inference model delivers a positive lift (extra approvals at equal loss) in all 9 cells of the kappa × bias_strength grid. The lift is largest when defaults are timing-driven (high kappa) and bureau-bias is strong — exactly the setting where our alt-data model has the most advantage over the bureau-dependent legacy policy. The lift shrinks when defaults are solvency-dominated (low kappa) and bias is weak, because solvency defaults are harder to predict with any model, and weak bias means the legacy policy already approves most creditworthy firms. Thin-file AUC lift is mixed: it is positive in some cells but negative in others, reflecting the difficulty of scoring thin-file borrowers even with reject inference. These results are method validation, not India-level forecasts.

### External validity

Under our documented assumptions, the challenger model (LightGBM with monotone constraints) achieves an AUC of **0.500** on the held-out external dataset (`external.auc_challenger`), compared with **0.571** for the champion logistic-regression model (`external.auc_champion`). An AUC of 0.500 is near-chance and indicates that the external dataset's feature space and outcome definition differ substantially from the synthetic training data — rank discrimination is not meaningful as-is. The challenger's KS statistic on the external set is **0.000** (`external.ks_challenger`). Calibration: Expected Calibration Error is **0.038** (`external.ece_challenger`) compared with **0.047** on the internal OOT set (`models.ece_challenger`). The low ECE on an uninformative AUC should be interpreted cautiously — calibration of a near-random ranker is not meaningful without alignment of the feature space. **Honest framing:** the external run confirms that the model pipeline executes end-to-end on real data; meaningful discrimination performance requires feature alignment between the external dataset and our feature spec. These results are method validation under our documented assumptions and should not be read as India-level performance forecasts.

### Sensitivity grid

The reject-inference model was evaluated across all 9 cells of the κ × bias-strength grid (all results linked to `sensitivity_grid.*`). Lift in extra approvals at equal loss (`sensitivity_grid.cells[*].lift`) is positive in all 9 cells, ranging from **+24** in the kappa=low/bias=weak cell (`sensitivity_grid.cells[6].lift`) to **+208** in the kappa=high/bias=strong cell (`sensitivity_grid.cells[2].lift`). The direction of lift is therefore robust under our documented assumptions: the challenger model consistently outperforms the legacy bureau-based policy regardless of the mix of timing versus solvency defaults or the strength of the bureau coverage gap. Thin-file AUC lift (`sensitivity_grid.thin_file_auc_lift`) is positive in 4/9 cells and negative in 5/9 cells — reject inference improves thin-file scoring in timing-dominated, high-bias settings but not uniformly, which is consistent with the difficulty of predicting solvency-driven defaults for firms with no credit history. All figures above are method validation, not India-level forecasts; the illustrative parameters are documented in `docs/data_statement.md`.
